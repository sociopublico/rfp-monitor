Proposal RequirementsTerms of Reference for External Evaluation of the Project - "Nothing About Us Without Us: Strengthening Refugee Leadership in Diplomacy" (Excerpt)Introduction:The SBK framework is a multi-year subsidy framework funded by the Ministry of Foreign Affairs of the Netherlands,aimed at strengthening locally led development through more equitable,inclusive, and balanced partnerships between international and local civil society organizations. This framework supports 10 so-called Support Partners(SPs) to work with In-Country Partners (ICPs) across diverse geographic and thematic contexts with a particular emphasis on shifting power, decision-making authority, resources, and visibility closer to local actors. The SPs have various roles with regards to the ICPs: 1) coach and co-learner, 2) connector and facilitator, 3) advocacy support, 4) soundboard and companion.Under the SBK framework, Support Partners implement portfolios of projects and programs across multiple countries and regions with budgets varying by Country and intervention. The SBK has three central intervention logics: capacity strengthening of in-country partners, direct funding of in-country partners, and meaningful participation of refugees or migrants in national and international policy influencing. Support Partners (SPs) can choose how to structure their portfolio based on ICP and community needs. Thus, the ICPs are not always connected to all three intervention strategies, which aligns with the flexibility the MFA aims to promote with this SBK. This way, the ICPs will grow within the fields that are most valuable to their specific organizational needs and be able to best tailor their activities to community needs. The SP portfolios typically include a combination of direct funding to ICPs, capacity strengthening support,partnership development, advocacy accompaniment, and learning-oriented approaches. Interventions are guided by overarching SBK principles, results framework and theories of change (if available) that aim to enhance the relevance, effectiveness, sustainability, and local ownership of development outcomes, while contributing to broader policy and practice learning on localization and equitable partnerships.The SBK framework involves a wide range of stakeholders, including the MFA, 10 Support Partners, ICPs,rights holders and constituencies, local and national authorities, and other civil society and knowledge actors. This evaluation is situated within this broader SBK framework and contributes to learning at both the framework level and the level of individual SP-implemented projects.This Expression of Interest relates specifically to the evaluation of the project "Nothing About Us Without Us: Strengthening Refugee Leadership in Diplomacy", implemented by INDEPENDENT DIPLOMAT (ID) in six countries in the East Africa and MENA regions. The project seeks to achieve the comprehensive and meaningful inclusion of refugees in refugee policymaking at the local, national, regional, and global levels, enabling lasting, systemic change that makes the refugee response system more effective and responsive to the needs of refugee communities,through three intervention strategies: (1) enabling local organizations, in particular refugee-led organizations, networks and advocates, to access the political space to make their voices heard and increase their operational impact; (2) strengthening refugee-led organizations' expertise and capacity to independently influence political negotiations and build policy coherence from the international to the local level; and (3) providing long-term, core funding to refugee-led organizations to ensure their ability to launch sustained policy and advocacy initiatives, ensure their operational resilience, and advance the self-reliance of refugees and host communities. While the project is implemented under the broader SBK framework,the primary focus of this evaluation is the performance and results of the “Nothing About Us Without Us: Strengthening Refugee Leadership in Diplomacy” project, rather than the SBK framework as a whole. Findings may contribute to broader learning on localisation and equitable partnerships, but the purpose of this evaluation is to assess project-level achievements, challenges, assumptions and lessons learned in order to inform adaptive management, future programming and organisational learning.‍Evaluation Purpose and Objectives:The evaluation will assess the relevance, coherence, effectiveness, efficiency and sustainability of the “Nothing About Us Without Us: Strengthening Refugee Leadership in Diplomacy” project, while examining the validity of the project's Theory of Change and underlying assumptions. Particular attention will be given to the extent to which the project has strengthened the capacities, autonomy and leadership of In-Country Partners (ICPs), enabled ICPs to deliver quality and contextually relevant results, and contributed to more equitable partnership practices in areas such as decision-making, funding, risk-sharing and learning. The evaluation will also generate evidence, lessons and recommendations to support accountability, organisational learning, adaptive management and future programming. ‍Scope of the Evaluation:The scope of the evaluation encompasses the stakeholder, temporal, geographical,programmatic, financial, and analytical dimensions of “Nothing About Us Without Us: Strengthening Refugee Leadership in Diplomacy”. The evaluation will assess project implementation and results throughout the evaluation period, including interventions, resource allocation, partnership arrangements, and contributions to intended outcomes. It will also examine the validity of the project's Theory of Change and underlying assumptions to understand the factors influencing implementation, effectiveness, learning, and sustainability.Stakeholder Scope:The evaluation should consider perspectives from:Support Partner staff: Independent Diplomat's program,diplomatic advisory, and MEL staff involved in designing and implementing the project's political access, capacity-building, and sub-granting activities; In-Country Partner staff and leadership: leadership and staff of refugee-led organizations (RLOs) supported under the project across the six target countries in East Africa and MENA, including both RLOs that received the full package of support (diplomatic access, capacity building, and funding)and those that received only a subset, where relevant to assessing the pathways described in the full ToR; Constituencies and rights holders: refugees of host communities represented or served by supported RLOs, including groups facing compounded marginalization (e.g., women and girls, unaccompanied children, LGBTQI individuals, indigenous people, and people with disabilities);Community representatives: leaders and members of refugee-led networks and coalitions engaged through the project, as relevant to the country contexts under review; Relevant government stakeholders: national government ministries, migration bureaus, and permanent missions of the six target countries, as well as regional and multilateral bodies engaged through the project's diplomatic strategy (e.g., UNHCR, the African Union, IGAD, and the MENA Civil Society Network for Displacement); Other strategic partners: civil society networks, faith-based and humanitarian organizations, and co-signatories of relevant policy commitments (e.g., the Refugee Participation Pledge, the UN Common Pledge 2.0) that collaborated with ID or its RLO partners in implementing the project.Temporal Scope:The evaluation shall cover the implementation period from 1 April 2024 to 31 December 2026. The evaluation should assess progress against intended outcomes and expected results during this period and, where relevant, consider significant contextual developments that may have influenced implementation, partnership dynamics and project performance.Geographical Scope:The evaluation will cover project activities implemented in six countries in the East Africa and MENA regions,including the specific regions, districts and localities in which the project and its In-Country Partners operate. The evaluation should consider contextual differences across locations and assess how these have influenced implementation processes, partnership arrangements and results.Programmatic Scope:The evaluation covers all interventions implemented under the “Nothing About Us Without Us: Strengthening Refugee Leadership in Diplomacy” project during the evaluation period, including activities delivered directly by the Support Partner and through In-Country Partners. This includes, where relevant:Capacity strengthening and organisational development;Direct funding and resource transfer mechanisms;Partnership development and accompaniment;Technical assistance and advisory support;Advocacy and influencing activities;Learning, reflection and adaptive management processes;Meaningful participation and engagement of constituencies and rights holders.The evaluation should assess both the implementation of these interventions and their contribution to intended project outcomes.Financial Scope:The evaluation covers expenditure and resources allocated to the “Nothing About Us Without Us: Strengthening Refugee Leadership in Diplomacy” project during the evaluation period, including activities delivered directly by the Support Partner during the evaluation period, including funding provided through the SBK framework and subsequently managed and disbursed by INDEPENDENT DIPLOMAT (ID). Particular attention should be paid to funding modalities, resource allocation practices, flexibility of funding arrangements,and their contribution to project implementation, organisational strengthening and sustainability of ICPs.Analytical Scope:The evaluation will assess the validity and performance of the project's Theory of Change and intervention logic. Particular attention should be given to the causal pathways linking:Political access and participation: facilitated RLO engagement in global, regional, and national policymaking forums and diplomatic processes, and the extent to which this engagement translates into RLOs' sustained ability to influence policy content and negotiation outcomes affecting refugees; Capacity strengthening: institutional accompaniment and the"Refugee Skill Up" training-of-trainers model, and the extent to which this translates into RLOs' independent institutional standing and demonstrated capacity to lead policy engagement without ID's direct facilitation;Core, long-term funding: sub-granting and financial literacy support to RLOs, and the extent to which this translates into RLOs' operational resilience, financial self-reliance, and ability to sustain policy and advocacy initiatives beyond the life of the grant. The analysis should examine each of these pathways individually against their intended outputs and outcomes, as well as how the three intervention logics interact and reinforce — or undermine — one another in practice, in line with the contribution analysis set out in the full ToR. This includes examining whether political access without adequate institutional or financial capacity results in participation that is more symbolic than substantive; whether funding and capacity gains are constrained where diplomatic access remains limited;and whether the combined, partner-led model produces effects — in terms of RLOs' self-reliant advocacy capacity — that would not have been achieved through any single intervention strategy alone.The evaluation should further consider how these causal pathways hold up differently across the project's varying country contexts, given the significant differences in civic space, political openness, and displacement dynamics, and should identify where contextual factors strengthen or weaken the plausibility of the project's overall theory of change.The evaluation should systematically assess the assumptions underpinning these pathways and determine whether these assumptions hold in practice, under what conditions they hold, and
