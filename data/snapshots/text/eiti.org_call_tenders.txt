We procure goods and services efficiently, transparently and ethically, securing best value for money while preventing conflicts of interest and corruption. Our procurement is governed by the EITI Procurement Policy and complies with Norway's Transparency Act (“Åpenhetsloven”). Procurement processCompetitive bidding is the default approach for procurement at the International Secretariat. Depending on value and complexity, opportunities are run as open (public) tenders, closed tenders among pre-qualified suppliers or framework agreements established competitively. Single-sourcing is used only in defined circumstances and requires documented justification and approval.Open opportunities are published on the EITI website and through our channels, with a minimum submission window of 10 business days (extended for high-value tenders). All bidders receive the same information at the same time: questions and clarifications are compiled and shared with every participant simultaneously.Bids are assessed by a procurement committee against evaluation criteria set out in advance in the tender documents. Technical and financial proposals are evaluated in two separate stages, with financial proposals opened only after the technical assessment is complete. Scoring, comments and the justification for the award are documented, and unsuccessful bidders are offered feedback. We manage conflicts of interest through mandatory declarations, recusal where conflicts arise and a conflict-of-interest registry. Anti-corruption standards apply to staff and suppliers alike, in line with the EITI Code of Conduct and the EITI Anti-Bribery and Anti-Corruption Policy. All procurement records are retained securely for a minimum of five years to support audit and accountability. Approval authority is governed by a defined Limit of Authority matrix. Human rights due diligenceAs a Norwegian-registered organisation, the EITI International Secretariat is subject to the Transparency Act. We carry out human rights and decent-working-conditions due diligence across our supply chain, following the OECD model of risk-based due diligence.In practice, this means a human rights risk assessment is carried out for suppliers and framework agreements, with enhanced due diligence for higher-risk engagements; human rights compliance forms part of supplier evaluation; and our contracts include clauses requiring suppliers to uphold human rights and decent working conditions, to permit audits, and to allow termination for non-compliance. Reflecting the Secretariat's size and the nature of its procurement, our due diligence focuses primarily on direct suppliers and extends further down the supply chain where the level of risk warrants.We publish an annual account of our human rights due diligence, as required by Section 5 of the Transparency Act. The statement describes our organisation and operations, the guidelines and procedures we use to manage risks to human rights and decent working conditions, the risks identified through our due diligence, and the measures taken to address them.
