Economists help shape decisions about jobs, public services, inequality and development. Yet women remain underrepresented in the profession, particularly at its most senior levels. A VoxDev series highlights research from our program partners working through the International Economic Association’s Women in Leadership in Economics initiative. Drawing on the first comparable, country-level evidence from Argentina, Colombia, Ghana, India, Mexico and South Africa, the latest article examines women’s representation across the academic economics pipeline. The findings show that the barriers women face differ significantly across countries. In some, women are well represented among economics students but face barriers as they move into academia. In others, the gap emerges much earlier. Across every country studied, however, women’s representation declines at higher academic ranks. 🧩 There is no one-size-fits-all solution. Creating a more inclusive economics profession requires understanding where barriers emerge in each context — and acting on that evidence. It recognizes more than a decade of collective work for gender parity in international justice and representation. International law and justice are central to how we build peace, defend democracy and rights, and respond to the climate emergency. Who gets to make those decisions matters. ⚖️ Women have an equal right to shape them. When women share power, institutions can draw on a wider range of experience to see whose rights are at risk and what remedies might work. That helps make them more legitimate and effective. Since GQUAL’s launch in 2015, the gender gap in the bodies we monitor has narrowed by 69%. Women have led international justice institutions and mechanisms for the first time, and bodies that had never reached parity have achieved and sustained it. International law has helped drive that change by clarifying the standard of gender parity in international leadership. These gains have many authors. Women have put themselves forward and broken barriers. Experts, advocates, civil society organizations, diplomats, and States have helped make underrepresentation harder to ignore and pushed for stronger rules and fairer selection processes 💜 👉 This award reinforces what we’ve learned together: Data makes exclusion visible; alliances keep up the pressure; legal standards give us a basis to demand change; and women need open pathways and support to thrive and lead. Yet much remains to be done.
